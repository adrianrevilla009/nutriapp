Topic: What is a micronutrient
Last reviewed: not yet reviewed (drafted 2026-09-07 as part of nutrition-assistant-service's implementation-plan resolution 2 -- a small, hand-authored, non-controversial seed corpus)

Micronutrients are vitamins and minerals the body needs in smaller amounts than macronutrients, but which are still essential for many bodily functions, including immune function, bone health, and energy metabolism. Common examples include vitamin C, vitamin D, calcium, iron, and potassium. Micronutrients are found across a wide variety of foods, particularly fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and animal products.
